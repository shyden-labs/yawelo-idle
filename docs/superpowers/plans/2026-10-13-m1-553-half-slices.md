# M1 #553: Each Golden Open Cut at Steps by Its Work, the Player Resumable Mid-Open Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** no unit test that replays or judges the golden log runs over 0.07 s of thread CPU at its heaviest, with every slice still in the unit suite (heavy ones in pacing, as #542 placed them), and the two first-test warm-ups (the golden fixtures' first read, verify-dev's first fetch) cut.

**Architecture:** the golden player's `open` becomes a generator that yields an `OpenStep` (the step it takes next, a review or pass count, and the open's end) before each step: collect, each review, the refusal, journeys, upgrades, grammar, automation and each purchase pass. A `PlayerStart` may carry that step, and a player built from it re-enters the open there. The recorder plays each open once, notes every step, and cuts it into `max(2, ceil(work / 4000))` parts, its work being its weight (each event weighs the hours its advance crosses, at least 1) times the words held as it begins. Cut `k` of `n` is the step nearest `k / n` of the weight such that every part judges every kind of shop offer or none. `golden-slices.jsonl` gains `part` and `until`, `golden-starts.jsonl` carries the step in each later part's start, and `golden-log.jsonl` stays byte for byte the same. Each slice parses only its own event lines; verify-dev's first fetch moves to file load.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Vitest, the repository's recorded floors (recorded in CI by the `record-floors` label, #547).

## Review Focus

1. **The resumable player plays as the whole one.** The recorder plays every open through the generator and writes the log; it is unchanged (`git diff --stat` lists no `golden-log.jsonl`). Each unit slice's test plays it again from its recorded start, mid-open for every later part, and must write exactly its events, end at the next slice's start (step included) and count what was recorded.
2. **Shop-free slices.** The shop is read before a resume and before each purchase (`SHOP_READ_BEFORE`). A part between purchases (reviews, listens, practice) reads none: measured, 24 of 380, and every other part judges every kind (no part judges only some). The shop test lists `SHOP_ROWS` (unit slices less the shop-free); each shop-free slice's test searches its events for a shop read, floored on their count, and checks its recorded shop counts are all zero; the recorder refuses a play whose shop-free slices differ from `GOLDEN_SHOP_FREE_SLICES`, and a unit test holds the list to the fixture. The whole log's judged totals still add up over the slices, and pacing still judges the whole log.
3. **The part count and cut points are a cost model, not a checked property.** #553 measured the cost as hourly bonus buckets rebuilt (about one an event, one for each hour a resume crosses), each reading every word held: work = weight × words correlates 0.58 with the measured heaviest test per open (weight alone 0.31, events 0.23). Slices of work 3,000 to 4,000 ran 46 ms at their heaviest, so the line is 4,000. Replay and shop tests load an open differently (the resume early, purchases late), so no cut balances both exactly; the CPU check enforces the result, and no test pins where an open is cut. `GOLDEN_OPEN_PARTS` (25 opens in 3 to 5 parts) is held to the fixture.
4. **The review and pass counts identify a stop.** An `OpenStep`'s count was first thought inert for reviews (it also feeds the 200-per-open cap, which no slice nears: 29 at most), but a part that starts and stops among the reviews finds its stop by comparing counts: mutation M3 reset it and exactly the 11 such parts went red.
5. **Warm-ups are cut by measurement, not by assertion.** Parsing only a slice's lines and the file-load fetch change no result; they are verified by the per-test CPU figures, and the line-number test covers the per-slice parser's errors.
